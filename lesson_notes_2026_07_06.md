## 📚 Vocabulary & Expressions

### Core Vocabulary
* **underdog** — The competitor nobody expects to win.
  * *Example:* "Mexico was the **underdog** against England — nobody thought they would win."
* **celebration** — A public party for a special event.
  * *Example:* "The 250-year **celebration** of the nation included a Sailor Parade."
* **Norwegian** — From or relating to Norway.
  * *Example:* "The **Norwegian** team is playing well this tournament."
* **internal clock** — Your body's natural sleep rhythm (often called a biological clock).
  * *Example:* "My **internal clock** wakes me up at 4 AM no matter what."
* **scholarship** — Money awarded for education that you don't pay back, usually based on academic or athletic skill.
  * *Example:* "She got a soccer **scholarship** to study in Florida."
* **grant** — Free money given by an organization or government for a specific purpose, which does not need to be repaid.
  * *Example:* "The government gave him a **grant** to start his business."
* **documented / registered (boat)** — Officially registered with the Coast Guard.
  * *Example:* "My boat is **documented** — I can't put it in the water until the papers renew."
* **enormous** — Extremely large.
  * *Example:* "An **enormous** category 5 hurricane hit Guam three days ago."
* **category** — A level on a scale of intensity.
  * *Example:* "What **category** was the hurricane? Category 5."
* **surgeon** — A doctor who performs medical operations.
  * *Example:* "If they want to be a **surgeon**, they have to go outside Puerto Rico."
* **investigation** — A formal review to find facts.
  * *Example:* "They have to order an **investigation** into the type of construction."
* **resilient** — Able to recover quickly from difficult conditions.
  * *Example:* "People in small towns are more **resilient** — they adapt when things go wrong."
* **novelty** — The quality of being new, original, or unusual.
  * *Example:* "If you can drink at the beach any day, there's no **novelty** — it's not special anymore."
* **infrastructure** — The basic physical systems of a country or area (e.g., roads, power, water).
  * *Example:* "After the earthquake, do they at least have **infrastructure** again — power, phones?"
* **specialty** — A focused area of expertise or study.
  * *Example:* "Once they hit a **specialty** like surgery, they have to go outside Puerto Rico to finish."
* **condominium** — A building containing individually owned apartments.
  * *Example:* "The **condominium** association buys a policy specifically for the building and the administration."
* **authorization** — Official permission to do something.
  * *Example:* "I don't have the **authorization** yet — the Coast Guard document is still being renewed."
* **worn out** — Completely exhausted or very tired.
  * *Example:* "I'm not just tired — I'm **worn out**."

### Phrasal Verbs
* **fill [someone] in on** — To update or bring someone up to date with information.
  * *Example:* "Has there been any exciting news you could **fill me in on** since last week?"
* **move out [of]** — To permanently leave a home (especially your parents' house).
  * *Example:* "At 18 years old, the first thing you want to do is **move out of** your parents' house."
* **grow up** — To spend one's childhood and youth.
  * *Example:* "We **grew up** in the same town — we've known each other forever."
* **pick up** — To collect someone or something in a vehicle.
  * *Example:* "I **pick up** my daughter to take her to school at 7 AM."
* **come back** — To return to a place.
  * *Example:* "How many of the students who leave Puerto Rico end up **coming back**?"
* **end up** — To arrive at a final situation or place, often unexpectedly.
  * *Example:* "Most students who study outside **end up** coming back to the island."
* **get by** — To manage to survive or cope with minimal resources.
  * *Example:* "After the earthquake, people were **getting by** — then they had to move to another town."
* **let [someone] down** — To disappoint someone by failing to meet expectations.
  * *Example:* "It really **let me down** — I expected a bigger celebration for the 250th anniversary."

### Key Collocations & Idioms
* **internal clock** — Your natural body rhythm.
* **wild card** — An unpredictable factor, variable, or person.
* **homeowners association / condominium board** — The group managing residential properties.
* **student loans / student debt** — Money borrowed for educational expenses.
* **summer camp** — A program for children during their school break.
* **Coast Guard** — Maritime law enforcement.

* **train of thought** — Your connected flow of ideas.

* **growth target / sales goal** — (Learner note: prefer these terms over *growth number*).

## ✍️ Grammar & Sentence Structure

### 1. Past Tense in Storytelling
When talking about completed past events, always use past-tense verb forms. This signals to your listener that the story is finished rather than happening right now.
* ❌ *Incorrect:* "We are grow up in the same town."
*  *Correct:* "We **grew up** in the same town."
* ❌ *Incorrect:* "I slept very bad last night."
*  *Correct:* "I **slept** very badly last night."
* ❌ *Incorrect:* "He have to move his family."
*  *Correct:* "He **had to** move his family to another town."

### 2. Introducing What Exists: "There is" & "There are"
In English, you must start sentences introducing facts or new objects with **"There is"** (singular) or **"There are"** (plural). Avoid translating directly from Spanish structures like starting with "Is..." or overusing "We have...".
* ❌ *Incorrect:* "Is some teams playing well."
*  *Correct:* "**There are** some teams playing well."
* ❌ *Incorrect:* "Is a big difference."
*  *Correct:* "**There is** a big difference in culture."
* ❌ *Incorrect:* "We have two or three medical schools on the island."
*  *Correct:* "**There are** two or three medical schools on the island." *(more natural for stating general facts)*

## 🔢 Takeaways: Expressing Numbers

Different contexts require specific prepositions or formatting:
* **Sports Scores:** Use **to** between numbers.
  * *Example:* "The final score was **2 to 3**." (not "2 a 3")
* **Time Ranges & Points:** Use **to** for ranges, **at** for specific points.
  * *Example:* "I slept for **5 to 6 hours**." / "My clock woke me up **at** 4 AM."
* **Scale Categories:** Place the word **Category** before the number.
  * *Example:* "It was a **Category 5** hurricane." (not "5 category")
* **Units of Time:** Place the number before the unit.
  * *Example:* "A **250-year** celebration." / "**3 weeks ago**" (past) / "**in 3 weeks**" (future).
* **Age Milestones:** Use **at** when referring to milestones.
  * *Example:* "He moved out **at 18 years old**."

## 🎯 Practice Activities

### In-Class Practice
1. **Score Descriptions:** Practice aloud describing sports match outcomes (e.g., *"Mexico 2, England 3"* or *"2 to 3"*).
2. **Storytelling Drill:** Describe a childhood memory or event using past-tense verbs like `grew up`, `went`, and `had to`.
3. **Number Drill:** Practice shifting between hours (*"5 to 6 hours"*), hurricane categories (*"Category 5"*), spans of years (*"250 years"*), and game scores (*"2 to 3"*).
4. **Starting Sentences:** Practice making general statements about your city or region using *"There is"* and *"There are"*.

### Self-Study Homework
* Watch a sports highlight and write down the final score, describing the game using the past tense.
* Write a paragraph describing your morning routine utilizing phrasal verbs: `wake up`, `get up`, `pick up`, and `drop off`.
* Tell a friend or record a voice note updating them on your week using the phrase *"fill you in on"*.
* Write 4 facts about your local culture or region, introducing each one with *"There is"* or *"There are"*.
