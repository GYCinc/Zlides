Grammar

[COUNT: N]

3rd person singular -s

After he/she/it, the verb takes -s. The #1 pattern for your professional accuracy.

ALTBase verb after I/you/we/they. Add -s/-es after he/she/it/the boss/the company.
EX"the part that put the people" → "the part that puts people"
EX"your boss talk to you" → "your boss talks to you"
TRAPThe -s drops in fast speech — pause and check after every he/she/it verb: boss talks, family has.

for + infinitive vs to + infinitive

Purpose after a verb needs "to", not "for". Spanish "para" transfers as "for receive" — English uses "to receive."

ALT"For" takes a noun ("for the claim"). "To" takes a verb ("to receive").
EX"for receive the claim" → "to receive the claim"
EX"for declare a person dead" → "to declare a person dead"
TRAPIf a verb follows the gap, it's "to", not "for" — every time.

Present perfect — the rope

"I have + verb" means the past is still tied to you, like a rope. I've read good books = they're still with me. I've lost my keys = it still affects me. Open box (still relevant) vs closed box (finished). Don't force it — notice every "I've" in the book and ask: is it still tied to me?
